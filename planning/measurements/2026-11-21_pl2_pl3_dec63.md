## 4. How the two ends are read, and the classes

`c52_pl23.ends` on the record in upper case (`contig_uid` is taken from the upper case too):

- **exact repeat**: the longest k from 20 (DEC-63's "20 bp or more") to half the record with the first k bases equal to
  the last k. Trimming it removes the last k bases: the circle then carries the repeat once and the record's start
  (and the source's coordinates from it) are unchanged.
- **further agreement of the ends**: any place within 10,000 bases of the end where the first 20 bases occur again, or
  within 10,000 bases of the start where the last 20 do, whose span is longer than the exact repeat and which is not
  itself an exact repeat. `near` when it looks like a second copy (identity ≥ 0.9 over its span: substitutions; or an
  unbroken run from the seed over half the span or more: an indel), else `far` (a shared stretch, then the ends
  differ: an internal repeat; counted, not acted on).
- **classes**: `exact` (an exact repeat of at most 1,000 bp, no near agreement, not low complexity: the trim is
  mechanical); `none` (no repeat, no near agreement: the record is the circle as it is); `partial` (a near agreement,
  with or without a shorter exact repeat); `long` (exact repeat over 1,000 bp, the bound this card set: the largest
  assembler k seen is 141, and every repeat of 1,000 bp or more is outside the k-mer overlaps); `low_complexity` (the
  exact repeat holds an N, has a period of 6 or less, or one base is 80% of it); `unresolved` (more than 1,000 places
  to look at: a low-complexity start).

## 5. What the code and the precedent say

**The code assumes a circular record holds the circle once.** `genecall._genes` calls a circular contig of length n
on `seq[n - flank:] + seq + seq[:flank]` and keeps genes whose start lies on the contig: with both copies of a k-bp
repeat in the record, the origin reads the repeat twice in a row, so a gene across the origin carries k extra bases
(an insertion, a frameshift unless k is a multiple of 3), and a gene inside the repeat is called once from each copy.
`crispr.py` searches a circular contig with `CIRC_FLANK` bases of the other end on each side and keeps an array whose
leftmost base is on the contig: an array inside the repeat is kept twice. `igr.py` and `tapes.py` take the last and
first features of a circular contig as neighbours across the origin with `contig_len` = n, k more than the circle.
Staged linear, the record keeps both copies but no join is read: a gene across the origin is split, nothing is
counted twice. So the code agrees with the orchestrator's reading as far as it goes: **circular is right only when the
repeat is there once**.

**The precedent never trimmed.** Card C-39 (`V1`, UHGV, built): a DTR record whose first 20 bases are found again in
its last 2,000 was listed **linear** (1,478 records), the 22,517 without circular (`2026-11-16_virus_votu_inputs.md`
§3). Card C-48c (`V2`, IMG/VR, staged): 59,502 of 59,608 DTR representatives keep the repeat and stay **linear**, by the
orchestrator's decision (2) ("trimming would change `contig_uid`"; STATUS row C-48c (2)). Card C-26 (`PL1`, PLSDB,
built): circular from `NUCCORE_Topology`, untrimmed; this card's survey finds 1,211 of `PL1`'s 62,797 circular genomes
still holding a repeat (523 exact, 217 long, 467 partial, 3 low complexity, 1 unresolved; *measured*, §4), so `PL1`
already has the duplicated origin the reading avoids. DEC-63 makes the plasmid stages differ from both: `IMG-PR`'s DTR
records become circular, where `V1` and `V2` keep the same kind of record linear.
