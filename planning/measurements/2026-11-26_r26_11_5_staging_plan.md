## 0. Summary

1. Of the 24 stages of DEC-79 (1) (`G-RARE1..3` counted as three), **4 have staged inputs** (`LR2`, `SAG1`, `NX1` by
   cards C-66 and C-58 on `/stor`, with metadata on `/tmp`; `GV1` on `/tmp` with a conf), **10 are on `tower`** and
   nowhere on the pod (`EGV1`, `ECE2`, `MIR1`, `ASG1`, `SAG2`, `DPN1`, `SYM2`, `LRP1`, `MIR2`, `MGE4`), **7 are in
   archives on the pod** that no card has cut yet (`G-RARE1..3` in GlobDB r226 on `/stor`, `MV2` and `MV-EUKV` in MetaVR on `/stor`, `KAV-HIFI` in the
   Kavagutti archive on `/tmp`, `INPHARED-2604` on `/stor`), and **3 are not fetched** (`GWV1`, `SAG3`, `WET1`, due on
   `tower` today from card C-72 part 1). The r232 and R232 tables are not on either host yet.
2. **Nothing moves from `tower` to the pod today: the tree has no script for that direction.** `relay_to_tower.sh`
   streams pod to `tower` only. A reverse relay is one small card (C-73); the alternative, a second fetch from each
   provider straight to the pod, needs entries and code paths that are not in `sources.yaml` for most of these sets
   (§6). Recommendation: the reverse relay for every set, about 25.2 GB and 43 minutes in all (*derived*, §6).
3. **Holds.** `SYM2` has no licence in any record (its job list says "open-public-NERSC", an access term): it moves
   to `r26.11.6` unless the PI reads one. The Bellas 2023 files of `MIR1` (figshare 21581355) have no provider read in
   any record. For the four batch-1 sets the records disagree (R-11 lists them as unconfirmed; the C-69 and C-70 prep
   notes recorded provider reads); the PI is asked to settle it (§10, Q1).
4. **Catches beyond §5.5 of the C-72 note**, found here by name and size (*inferred*, to be read by the cards):
   `MGE4`'s three FASTA files are named like lab strains of one conjugation experiment ("Donor_strain", "Transconjugant",
   about 4.5 to 5.1 MB each), not a catalogue of elements; `SYM2`'s 2.79 GB `assemblies_fna.tar.gz` and `MIR2`'s 831 MB
   `protist_genomes_assembled.tar.gz` are protist host assemblies, eukaryotic and for card C-60, not for this release;
   `ASG1`'s `Data.zip` (4.96 GB) and `DPN1`'s `Nanobdellota_Zen.zip` (9.01 GB) are 3 to 30 times larger than their stated
   genomes. Four set names (`G-RARE1..3`, `MV-EUKV`, `KAV-HIFI`, `INPHARED-2604`) are not valid stage names (the driver's
   `^[A-Z][A-Za-z0-9]{0,15}$`); §4 proposes `GRARE1..3`, `MVEUKV`, `KAVHIFI`, `INPH2604`.
5. **The dedup against GlobDB r232 can be by accession only in this release**: neither the r232 genome archive (DEC-79
   (2)) nor GTDB R232's genomes are fetched, so a species-level (ANI) test is possible only against GlobDB r226 on
   `/stor`. The first r232 cut (`G232-A`, `r26.11.6`) then has to dedupe against `r26.11.5`'s stages (§10, Q5).
6. **Cost:** the 24 stage builds come to about 14,000 to 24,000 CPU-minutes for the steps `stage_est.py` prices, most
   of it Pfam on the annotated sets (*derived*, §3, §4); staging itself is light (at most 8 threads per card, a few hours
   each, *estimate*), but it runs beside the build of `r26.11.4`, whose Pfam shards passed the 280 CPU-minute guard when
   the pod ran more threads than its 56 physical cores (STATUS W1). **Rule proposed:** staging plus build at most 56
   threads, at most two staging cards computing at once, none by weekday day hours while the build holds the 40-thread
   day cap.
7. **`/tmp`:** 427 GiB free now (*measured*). `r26.11.4`'s staged inputs hold about 158 GB (*derived*, §7) and stay
   until that release is verified (DEC-66 S-3); its release train needs 230 to 280 GB of scratch (DEC-77 (1)). So every
   large staged file of `r26.11.5` goes to `/stor/scratch` (18 TB free) and comes into `/tmp/if2453/s2/inputs/` only in
   card A-29, after `r26.11.4` is verified.
8. **Cards** (§9): C-73 (relay), C-74 (r232 tables and an accession index), C-75 (`GRARE1..3`), C-76 (`LR2`, `SAG1`,
   `NX1`), dated lines on C-69 (`EGV1`) and C-70 (`ECE2`, `MIR1`, `ASG1`), C-77 (`SAG2`), C-78 (`DPN1`, `SYM2`, `LRP1`,
   `MIR2`, `MGE4`), C-79 (`MV2`, `MVEUKV`), C-80 (`KAVHIFI`, `INPH2604`), C-81 (`GWV1`, `SAG3`, `WET1`), A-29 (the chain).

`STEP_OVERHEAD` 1.23, `CRISPR_CPU_S_PER_GBP` 168.1, `SCAN_CPU_MIN_PER_M` 7.96). Other steps (IGRs, tapes, dimensions,
checks, backups) are not priced, so every figure below is a lower bound of the build's CPU. Per new Gbp:

## 6. How the sets on `tower` reach the pod

**The two ways.**
- **(A) Relay from `tower` to the pod through the laptop.** The tree has only the other direction:
  `relay_to_tower.sh` (card C-49) reads the pod (`stat`, `cat`) and writes `tower` (`tee .partial | sha256sum`, `mv -n`).
  Its rate is 9.70 MB/s for one stream (*measured*, card C-49, quoted in the script's header). The reverse needs a script
  (an agent may not run `ssh tower cat | ssh pod tee` by hand: AGENTS §1.8 forbids pipes): card C-73, the same design
  with the roles swapped, a destination on the pod under `/tmp/if2453/` or, for files of 64 MB and more only,
  `/stor/scratch/Wilke/if2453/bio-box-staging/` (AGENTS §7.3), and the sha256 of `tower`'s `SHA256SUMS` as the record.
  Gains: the provider is asked once; the pod's bytes are checked against the sha256 that `tower` took at the provider's
  md5; it serves every later set on `tower` too (`LR3` in `r26.11.6`: `MFD_LR` alone is 20.79 GB). Cost: the laptop
  must stay on while it streams.

  GlobDB r226's 251.37 GB in 3 h 46 min (about 18.5 MB/s, *derived* from `globdb_r226.fetched`), so (B) is faster per

  `c70_tier1_mge_dsdna_asgard.py` also call the Zenodo and figshare record APIs, which no entry lists, and expect
