# Search recipes from the 2017-2021 slice

Status: literature extraction for the search-infrastructure round (2026-10-29). The companion table is `recipes_2017_2021.tsv` (197 rows, 16 columns, schema and question-type codes from `00_brief.md`). Paths below are relative to `../bioinformatics-synteny/`. Evidence is `file:line`; for the image-only Doron PDF it is `pdf:p<page>` from an OCR pass noted in the row.

## 1. What the slice contains, and what is wrong with it

77 file stems start with 2017-2021. Two pairs are duplicates (the two `2020_Price_..._GapMind_*` stems are byte-identical text; `2018_Greenlee_...` holds the same paper as `2021_Brewer_Greenlee_...`), leaving 76 `paper_id` values and 75 distinct papers.

**The file names are not reliable: roughly 25 stems hold a different paper than the name says.** The table keeps the stem as `paper_id`, records the real year in `year`, and names the real paper in `seed` or `curation`. Three categories:

- *Wrong paper, nothing extractable* (one `Q-OTHER` placeholder row each, so the gap stays visible): Ellens 2017, Hudson 2017 RODEO-thiopeptide, Burroughs 2019, **Cohen 2019 CBASS** (all three files hold an unrelated translation-initiation paper), Rodionov 2019 (its supp dir does hold the real `Table_1-7.xlsx`), Ferlez 2019, Sherlock 2019 ykkC-2c, both Andreas/Giessen 2021 stems, Brewer 2021 NAR, **Tal 2021 Thoeris**.
- *Wrong paper, but one with real methods, extracted under the stem id*: `2020_Gao_Makarova_Koonin_Zhang_Retrons_RADAR_DRT_Supplement.txt` is two concatenated 2022 papers, Gao/Altae-Tran **Avs** (`txt:1-135`) and **Rousset, phage-satellite hotspots of antiviral systems** (`txt:137-620`) — this is where the Rousset recipe lives, 7 rows; `2019_Strecker_...CAST` is Kannan 2022 compact Cas13 (the 3.17 Tbp / 10-billion-ORF recipe); `2020_Jimmy_...toxSAS` is the Panacea preprint, while its supp dir holds the genuine toxSAS PNAS supplement (both extracted); `2021_Kurata_...` is the RSH-toxin preprint; `2019_Patz_...` is Rojas-Rojas; `2020_Sherlock_...Guanidine_Orphan` is McCown/Breaker 2017; `2020_Panchapakesan_...nadA` is Hamal Dhakal 2023; `2021_Sutter_...68_BMC_Loci` text is the 2022 Frontiers carboxysome review while its supp dir holds the intended 2021 Nat Commun BMC catalog (including its HMM libraries); both GapMind stems are GapMind **for carbon sources** (2022).
- *Supp dir belongs to another paper*: Benler 2021, Ozcan 2021, Li 2021 CreTA (`gkac712` is the 2022 follow-up), Koonin 2017 (the `12862_...docx` is the BMC article, not the Annual Review text).

Two text-recovery problems worth recording for the pipeline: **Doron 2018 is an image-only PDF** (`.txt` holds only page furniture), so its methods were recovered by `pdftoppm -r 200 -gray` plus `tesseract --psm 1`, with every threshold cross-checked in three places because OCR renders `>=` as `=>` or `2`; and several key papers are author manuscripts whose **supplementary Materials and Methods are simply absent from the corpus** (Altae-Tran OMEGA, Gao 2020, Yan 2019, Faure 2019, Makarova 2020, Shmakov 2017), which is why those rows carry tool names with `not stated` parameters.

Readings that matter for the plan:

- **Neighborhood work and nucleotide-level work together are a third of all rows** (65 of 197), and they outnumber family census (28) and iterative expansion (18) combined. - **Q-STRUCT is effectively absent (1 row), and there is no Foldseek and no AlphaFold anywhere in the slice.** Structure enters as RaptorX / Phyre2 / I-TASSER / trRosetta modelling plus DALI or PDBeFold searches (Mestre 2020, Kaur 2020, Panacea, Pinilla-Redondo, Ofir 2018, Zaremba 2017). This slice is the pre-AF2 boundary; the 2022-2026 slice is where that changes.
- **Q-REMOTE drops to zero in the 2020 and 2021 rows as a primary code**, not because profile-profile search stopped but because HHpred moved from being the search to being a curation step inside `Q-NBR` and `Q-CLUST` rows (Benler's HHblits-then-HHsearch ladder, Avs cluster representatives, Gussow's HHblits hit-count filter).

## 3. The recipe patterns, as step sequences

Six patterns cover nearly every row. Each is written as the step chain actually run, with the papers that instantiate it.

### P1. Defense-island / guilt-by-association screen (the dominant pattern)

`known-family positive set -> precomputed domain annotation of all genomes -> +-N gene window around every member -> score fraction of members with a positive-set neighbour -> threshold -> cluster the window contents -> conserved-cassette graph -> manual triage -> experiment`

- Doron 2018: 123 positive pfams; `+-10` genes; defense score = fraction of pfam members with a defense neighbour; context variability score = unique neighbour-lists / total lists; keep `defense score >= 65%` and `variability >= 0.1`; 14,083 pfams scanned -> 277 pass -> 335 candidates; then OrthoMCL v2.0.9 (`-F 'm S' -v 100000 -b 100000 -e 1e-5 -m 8`, mcl inflation 1.1) per anchor over the 21-gene windows, keep the 10% largest clusters and 10% thickest adjacency edges, maximal path through the anchor = candidate system, merge variants sharing `>=50%` of clusters, require `>=5` appearances in different species; two full cycles, the second seeded with the families found in the first (`pdf:p6`, `pdf:p7`).
- Gao 2020: all genes `within 10 kb or 10 open reading frames` of a known defense system -> 8.7e6 candidates -> 6e5 seeds; seed kept if a high fraction of its homologs are defense-proximal and the contexts are diverse; the operative thresholds survive only in the figure legend (`minimum 50 homologs`, frequency `0.1`) (`txt:79`, `txt:133`, `txt:242`).
- Millman 2020 and Bernheim 2021 are the compact Sorek form: cluster all proteins of 38,167 IMG genomes, defense score per cluster over a `10 genes upstream and downstream` window, then a 5-gene window for cassette structure. Bernheim adds the scaffold gate (`at least ten genes from each side`), which costs roughly half the cluster (1394 -> 735 scorable in one case) (`2020_Millman...txt:2366-2395`, `2021_Bernheim...txt:8`, `:341-357`).
- Shmakov 2018 and the Shmakov 2019 Nature Protocols write-up are the statistically explicit version (P2 below).
- Mestre 2020 is the same pattern with a real null model: all ORFs `+-30 kb` of 9141 RT representatives, MMseqs2 connected-component then cascaded profile clustering to convergence, presence/absence matrix ordered by the RT tree, moving average `bandwidth = 50, steps = 1`, normalised against `10 000 random permutations for each cluster size`, significance at `the mean value plus four times the standard deviation`, `phyvalue` = ratio to that cutoff, keep `>= 2`, split subclusters at `>10 positions below the cutoff`; then a second, orthogonal guard: the RTs carrying a subcluster must be under `60%` identical to each other, which cut 131 subclusters to 71. Negative control = group II introns; positive control = RT-CRISPR/Cas2 (`txt:147`, `:155-176`, `:912`).

### P2. Icity / CRISPRicity (bait -> vicinity -> permissive cluster -> whole-DB profile search -> relevance metrics)

`bait family or non-coding feature -> extract +-10 kb vicinity -> MMseqs2 permissive clustering (0.3 id / 0.1 cov) -> purge each cluster by PSI-BLAST of its own profile against its own members, 3-5 times -> psiblast each cluster profile against the whole protein DB with -dbsize 20000000 -> icity = effective-in-vicinity / effective-total -> median gene distance -> effective abundance -> voxel or threshold selection -> CDD + HHpred curation`

This is the most reusable recipe in the slice and it is published as a protocol with three worked datasets. Verbatim search line (`2019_Shmakov...txt:1144`): `psiblast -db <Database> -outfmt "7 qseqid sseqid slen sstart send evalue qseq sseq qstart qend score" -seg no -evalue <Evalue> -dbsize 20000000 -max_target_seqs 10000 -comp_based_stats no`. Window is set in base pairs (`NeighborhoodVicinitySize 10000`) with the guidance `one to two protein-coding genes or 1-2 kilobase pairs` for TA-size systems and `10-15 genes or 10-20 kbp` for CRISPR-size operons (`:171`, `:926`). Thresholds: icity `> 0.6` and distance `< 5` for the CRISPR set, or icity `> 0.7`, effective distance `< 2`, effective abundance `>= 1`; alternatively a voxel partition keeping the sector with the best F0.5 (reported F0.5 0.98, precision 0.98, recall 0.96) (`:636`, `:1318`, `:1347`, `:1466`). The 2018 ancestor uses a 10x10x10 voxel grid over CRISPRicity, log-abundance (step 0.3 dex) and gene distance, keeping voxels with `I = Dc/Dn > 2`, and concedes `the absence of rigorous statistical framework` (`2018_Shmakov...txt:1173-1193`, `:1105`).

### P3. Array-first or feature-first discovery (no protein seed at all)

`call repeats or non-coding features over all contigs -> take proteins within N kb -> reject loci by composition rules -> expand the surviving families by BLAST/PSI-BLAST -> require the family to stay near the feature`

- Smargon 2017: PILER-CR on Ensembl Release 27 genomes; proteins within `10 kb` of an array; reject a locus `if more than one protein of size 700 amino acids or larger or if either Cas1 or Cas2 were present`; candidate effectors are then the 900-1800 aa proteins that remain; expand by BLASTP vs nr at `E 1e-7`; keep a family only with `>=10 candidate effectors and >50% of them within 10 kb of a CRISPR array`. A second pass goes accessory-first: find Csx27/Csx28, then look `within 1 kb` for new effectors (`txt:1382-1389`).
- Shmakov 2017 runs two seeds in parallel, a Cas1 profile against translated genomic and metagenomic sequence and the array itself, and pools Piler-CR with CRISPRfinder (47,174 arrays, `more than twice the number of cas1 genes`). Its instructive move is the `>500 aa` effector gate, later removed on purpose to recover ~400-aa TnpB-like effectors (`txt:475-490`, `:1620-1626`).
- Li 2021 CreTA is the purest case: the element has no ORF, so the search is `500 bp each side of every cas6 gene`, `blastn -task blastn-short` with the genome's own CRISPR repeat as query, keep loci with a single repeat-like hit and discard real arrays, then a regex for the 5'-handle `NTTGAAGN` with yR at 60-80% identity to the co-occurring repeat and a target match required before a hit is called (`txt:1279`, `:1292`).

### P4. Six-frame / re-called-ORF rescue inside a defined region

`define a region (BGC, locus, island, IGR, bin) -> translate it in all six frames or re-call ORFs -> search the translations -> keep hits that are not annotated CDS`

Nine `Q-NT` rows in 2017 alone. Tietz 2017 six-frame-translates the IGRs of 1,419 BGCs into 25,506 candidate peptides because `~85%` of the final precursors `were not recognized as coding sequences in GenBank`, after BLAST on known lasso peptides had returned `<300 hits` cumulatively (`txt:11`). Shakya 2017 translates its GTA-like regions `in their entirety into all six reading frames` to recover decaying genes. Zaremba 2017 translates genome bins in six frames with Asgard-specific ribosomal profiles to recover missing r-proteins. Yutin 2018 TBLASTNs the MCP set into wgs/nr and translates the hits in six frames, keeping `either complete proteins or fragments longer than 150 aa`. Shmakov 2020 inverts the logic: it six-frame-translates candidate CRISPR arrays and annotates the ORFs to detect repeats that are really coding sequence (`Isolated arrays >400 bp and ORF overlap >0.95 were filtered out`). Benler 2021 and Ozcan 2021 skip deposited gene calls entirely (Prodigal v2.6.3 on raw WGS/MG-RAST contigs; MetaGeneMark on NCBI/JGI/ENA), and Kannan 2022 does it at corpus scale: `3.17 trillion bp`, `All open reading frames larger than 80 aa were annotated resulting in 10 billion putative proteins`.

### P5. The IGR / structured-RNA pipeline

`extract IGRs corpus-wide -> select a subset that concentrates the rare class -> BLAST-cluster IGR subsequences -> CMfinder -> score (RNAphylo + hmmpair percentiles, or R-scape) -> manual covariation curation -> cmbuild/cmsearch to expand -> re-align -> R2R -> tabulate the downstream gene`

Weinberg 2017 is the reference implementation and the only paper in the slice that states corpus-wide IGR scale: `237 Gb` of sequence containing `37 Gb of IGRs`, with the explicit reason for subsetting being that `the false positive rate when IGRs are compared increases with their total number`. Strategies and counts: 153 lineage/environment subsets, 1 large-IGR run (IGRs `>=600 bp`, 1.4 Gbp input), 47,831 downstream-gene clusters, 7 element-adjacent runs. Scoring is percentile-based within ~250,000 reference alignments, final score = minimum of the RNAphylo and hmmpair percentiles. RNAcode is run as a coding-sequence control (best P ~0.001, `With 224 motifs, this corresponds to an E-value of ~0.22, which is not convincing`). Brewer 2021 is the small form (26 genomes, manual %GC-vs-length selection polygon, BLASTX against nr to drop `Known ORF` cases, Infernal against a pre-extracted RefSeq 80 IGR database, CMfinder refinement `After one or more cycles`). Mestre 2020 adds R-scape to `reduce the relative weight of phylogenetic correlations and base composition biases not due to conserved RNA structure`. McCown/Breaker runs Infernal deliberately loose (`an E-value for possible representatives of as high as 10,000 were considered`) and then culls by five manual rules.

### P6. Catalog with a fixed model library, plus a rule layer

`curated HMM library -> hmmsearch with model-specific gathering cutoffs -> locus scoring / co-occurrence rule -> clustering of loci -> tree -> published HMM library`

Benler 2021 (Pfam GA cutoffs for curated models, `-T 25 -domT 25 -incT 25 -incdomT 25` for custom ones, TnsB bitscore `>=60`), Sutter 2021 BMC catalog (BMC type called where `both the BMC-H and BMC-P of a certain type are found as top hits of an HMM search`, then locus scoring and MCL), toxSAS (per-subfamily HMMs over 24,072 proteomes, then a phylogeny-driven subfamily revision and a full re-scan: `The 35615 sequences in the MySQL database were re-scanned with the updated subfamily HMMs`), ARTS 2017 (TIGRFAM v15 + Resfam + Pfam DUFs with trusted cutoffs and a 50% coverage gate on both model and gene), Price 2018/2020 (Pfam 28.0 / TIGRFAM 15.0 trusted cutoffs; GapMind's ublast/HMMer rule layer), Rousset 2022 (MacSyFinder 1.0.2 with per-profile score thresholds 32/27/27 and 80/20, plus the `loner` parameter).

## 4. Recurring parameter conventions

- **PSI-BLAST at `E 1e-4` with a pinned effective database size.** The Koonin-lab convention appears verbatim across the whole slice: `e-value cutoff of 10-4 and effective database size set to 2 x 10^7` (Shmakov 2018, four times), `-dbsize 20000000` (Shmakov 2019 protocol, Yan 2018 prophage search), `the database size set 2x10^6 (to eliminate the impact of small database size on e-values)` (Yutin 2018 supplementary table). Exploratory searches relax to `0.01` with `composition based statistics and low complexity filtering turned off` (Shmakov 2018, Makarova 2019, He 2018). **Nobody in the slice states `-Z` for HMMER**, and no paper outside the Koonin lab pins a database size at all — so E-values are not comparable across these papers.
- **Bit-score thresholds instead of E-values where database size is unstable**: HMMER3.2 `minimum bit-score threshold of 25` over 3.17 Tbp (Kannan), `-T 25 -domT 25 -incT 25 -incdomT 25` (Benler), model-specific gathering or trusted cutoffs (Benler, ARTS, Price, Infernal/Rfam v14.5 in Benler).
- **Iteration is almost never convergence-based.** Fixed round counts: `maximum of 6 iterations` (Shakya), `three iterations` with `The 2,000 best hits from each run were taken` (Avs), `4 iterations` with `Only hits with >70% coverage and e-value <10-8` (Pinilla-Redondo Aca5) dropping to `up to three iterations, only considering hits with e-values <10-4` for the Acr searches, `default parameters (3 iterations)` (Bernheim MMseqs2), `-num_iterations 10` (Doron), `typically run up to five iterations` (Kaur). True convergence statements: `until convergence` (Gussow, He, Yutin at `5-6 iterations`, Mestre's cascaded MMseqs2). Only two papers state a semantic stopping rule: Tietz (`until no new lasso peptide-like BGCs were returned`, then re-seeded with representatives from six further phyla to break the Actinobacteria/Proteobacteria bias) and Benler (HHblits `terminated if the number of effective sequences in the alignment was greater than 10`).
- **Curation between rounds is manual and is stated as such**: `Manual curation of clusters` as a numbered pipeline step (Shmakov 2017), `manually reviewed each resulting alignment` and removal of `partial false-positive hits to very large proteins` (Gussow), Pfam models `corrected for their boundaries where required and augmented by addition of newly detected divergent members` (Kaur), `no preset cutoffs were used in database searches, and all search results and alignments were examined individually for conservation of diagnostic sequence motifs` (Yutin), `After a round of curation to remove false positives hits and partial proteins, referencing the corresponding genome assemblies to correct misannotated start codons` (Avs).
- **Neighborhood windows are purpose-sized, not conventional.** Gene counts: `+-10` genes (Doron, Shmakov 2018/2019, Millman, Bernheim, Gussow, EFI-GNT default, Nichols), 5 genes (Millman cassette step), 6 genes either side of the cyclase (RODEO), `30 genes upstream and downstream` (Ofir class definition), 40-ORF flanks for decay measurement and a `<15 ORFs` merge gap (Shakya). Base pairs: 500 bp (Li), 600 bp flanks (Shmakov 2020 leaders), 1 kb effector-to-accessory and 10 kb effector-to-array in the same paper (Smargon), 10 kb vs 20 kb asymmetric by target in the same paper (Peters: 10 kb to span an I-F system, 20 kb to span a Tn7 element), 10 kb (Gao, Doron thsB, Sutter cassette), 25 kb (Pinilla-Redondo manual scan), 30 kb (Mestre), 125 kb (Benler motif window). Two papers measure distance in genes but set the window in bp (Shmakov 2018/2019), which only works with a protein-to-coordinate map.
- **Strand rules are rare and worth copying when present**: Gussow's unit is a directon, `consecutive proteins on the same strand with a maximum of 100 bp between adjacent proteins`, and the HTH must be `downstream of and adjacent to (within three genes)` inside it; Benler's operon is `two codirected ORFs separated by less than 50 bp of noncoding sequence`; Kaur requires `conservation of gene directionality within the neighborhood` plus a `generally 70 nucleotides` distance; Panacea requires `a maximum distance of 100 nucleotides between the two genes`; Brewer takes `The first gene downstream of each representative of a motif` as the regulated gene; Smargon takes repeat orientation from the effector gene. BiG-SCAPE explicitly does the opposite: in glocal mode `strandedness is not considered`.
- **Contig-edge handling, where stated at all**: `IGRs within 800 bp of the end of a contig could be misannotated if a truncated gene was not detected, and so these IGRs were also discarded` (Weinberg, stated twice, plus IGRs containing any N dropped); `located less than 3 kb from the contig boundary were discarded to remove incomplete transposons` (Benler, which also drops 171 of 523 benchmark CASTs as possibly contig-spanning); `at least ten genes from each side` on the scaffold (Bernheim); `>100 scaffolds` genomes removed, then genes `located at the end of a scaffold` removed (Sutter); loci `across two contigs` discarded (Rousset); `ni: no information (contig too short or RT in 5' or 3' contig end)` recorded as missing rather than absent (Mestre); `contig truncated upstream to drmA` as an explicit class-unclear reason in 31 rows (Ofir supp table S2); `extends up until the end of the BGC or a contig break in the genome assembly` (BiG-SCAPE glocal); contigs `at least 5kb in length` and clusters spanning fewer than 3 contigs (Yan); `the gene encoding the GRE is at the end of a sequencing contig so genome context is available in only one direction` (EFI-GNT, as a documented explanation of missing neighbours). Everyone else is silent, and silence here is a replay hazard.
- **Clustering thresholds in use**: BLASTCLUST 90% id / 0.9 cov (Peters, Silas, Yutin), UCLUST 0.3 / 0.5 / 0.9 for permissive / single-linkage / effective-size counting in one paper (Shmakov 2018), MMseqs2 at 0.3/0.1, 0.4/0.8, 0.5/0.5, 0.5 id / 0.7 cov then 0.9/0.8, 0.75, 0.8 id / 0.75 cov cascaded to 0.5, 0.9, 0.95/0.8 with `--cov-mode 1`, `-c 0.6`, and `-s 7.5` with 60% coverage (Shmakov 2019, Avs, Rousset, Kannan, Mestre, Benler, Millman, Bernheim), CD-HIT 85% (EFI-CGFP), OrthoMCL/MCL inflation 1.1 and 1.2 (Doron, Shakya), dRep `99% ANI across 95% of contig length` (Benler).
- **The profile-tree idiom**: MUSCLE or MAFFT within clusters, HHsearch or HHalign between cluster profiles, bit-scores symmetrised and converted to a pseudo-distance, UPGMA dendrogram, sequence trees grafted on and midpoint-rooted. It recurs in Peters 2017, Silas 2017, Benler 2021 and Kannan 2022 with nearly identical wording (Kannan states it as `sij = (sij + sji)/2` and `dij = -(log sij - log min(sii,sjj))/2`), plus the alignment-column filters `gap fraction >0.5` and `homogeneity <0.1`.

## 6. What runs on web servers

Per-row, the non-local steps cluster into five services, and every one of them is doing a job the local corpus could do better:

- **HHpred / the MPI Bioinformatics Toolkit** is the single most-used external service: Shmakov 2017, Smargon 2017, Zaremba 2017, Tietz 2017, Doron 2018, Yutin 2018, He 2018, Shmakov 2018/2019 (as the named curation stage), Makarova 2019, Avs, Rousset, Millman, Panacea, Pinilla-Redondo, Gao 2020, Mestre 2020, Benler 2021; Bernheim runs `HMMER 3.2.1 in the MPI bioinformatics toolkit` with 205 non-redundant seeds against nr eukaryotes. Benler's ladder (CDD PSI-BLAST -> HHblits vs UniClust30 June 2020 requiring `any database sequence align with >=20% of the query` -> HH-suite vs PDB+CDD, accept probability `>90`) is the fullest local-equivalent spec in the slice.
- **IMG as a queried service rather than a corpus**: Doron and Ofir join IMG's precomputed Pfam annotations on locus_tag; Sutter's entire survey is a set difference between an IMG `find functions` query (PF12288 or PF08936) and an IMG `cassette search` for `10 kb regions of genome sequence encoding members of` six Pfams; Rousset uses IMG's built-in blastp (December 2020) and Millman uses IMG annotations for neighbour identity.
- **EFI-EST / EFI-GNT / EFI-CGFP**: Tietz 2017 (per-class alignment-score cutoffs 1E-1 precursors, 1E-5 RREs, 1E-7 leader proteases, 1E-25 cyclases), Zallot 2019 (the tool paper: UniRef90 above 25,000 sequences, UniRef50 above 100,000 clusters; GNT window `+-10 ORFs` with co-occurrence frequency default 20%, set to 10%), Nichols 2021 (`an E value of 1E-5 and a maximum of 10,000 sequences`, then GNT `to identify the 10 genes upstream and downstream of every Family 2 encapsulin hit`). The 10,000-sequence cap and the 256 GB-RAM Cytoscape desktop that Zallot reports for a 20,232-sequence SSN are the two hard ceilings a local store removes.
- **Structure prediction and structure search**: RaptorX, Phyre2, I-TASSER, trRosetta, then DALI server and PDBeFold (Mestre, Kaur, Panacea, Pinilla-Redondo, Ofir, Zaremba). Plus JPred/Jpred4, TMHMM 2.0, SignalP, Phobius, ConSurf.
- **Everything RNA and CRISPR that is not Infernal**: RNAfold and the WAR server (Millman), BPROM (Millman), CRISPRTarget and CRISPRminer v2 (Smargon, Pinilla-Redondo), PHASTER (Silas, toxSAS via URLAPI, Pinilla-Redondo), GREMLIN (Tietz, Ekiert), the JGI taxonomy server (Doron), CIPRES for RAxML/IQ-TREE (toxSAS), NCBI web BLASTp (Nichols, `BLASTp searches were carried out in February 2020`), Geneious Prime for manual context viewing (Gussow, Peters).

Three whole papers are web tools whose value is precomputation, and they state the argument explicitly: ARTS (`the analysis took 15 min using the same resources`), EFI (the point is that experimentalists should not need a cluster), GapMind (30 s per genome, engineered with top-4-candidates-per-step and sqlite3 instead of flat files).

## 7. Operations a local corpus-wide store would have to serve fast

Ordered by how often they recur across the 197 rows.

1. **"All genes within N genes or N kb of every member of family X, corpus-wide, with strand and contig-edge flags."** 37 rows depend on it. It must accept the window in either unit, return distance in genes *and* bp, mark neighbours that fall off a contig edge as missing rather than absent (Mestre's `ni`), and expose the per-gene strand and intergenic gap so directon and codirected-operon rules can be applied on top (Gussow 100 bp same-strand, Benler 50 bp codirected, Kaur 70 nt, Panacea 100 nt).
2. **A protein-to-contig-coordinate map, and the reverse.** Required by Ekiert (restricting a coevolution alignment to `genes found within the same operon (maximum of two genes apart in the same contig)`, resolved by mapping UniProt IDs back to ENA contigs), Shakya (hits mapped to source genomes via `release76.AutonomousProtein2Genomic.gz`), Shmakov 2018/2019 (distance in genes over a bp window), and every neighborhood row.
3. **Corpus-wide clustering at several identity levels, kept simultaneously.** The icity family needs permissive (0.3/0.1) clusters for family definition *and* strict (0.9) clusters for effective-size counting in the same query; Doron needs per-species deduplication of both cluster sizes and edge weights; Gussow needs family/subfamily weight splitting; Shmakov 2020 needs genome weights from a UPGMA tree of shared-cluster fractions. All of these are clonal-inflation corrections that are only cheap if the clustering is precomputed.
4. **"Fraction of a cluster's members that sit near a member of positive set P"**, with P editable at query time. Doron, Gao, Millman, Bernheim and Shmakov all compute this; Millman's detail is that the positive set must be editable because clade scores are computed `while removing the effector genes from the positive set to avoid artificial inflation of the scores`.
5. **A permutation or resampling null over a presence/absence matrix ordered by a tree, and length-matched random genomic loci.** Mestre (10,000 permutations per cluster size, moving average bandwidth 50), Benler (`a random selection of 100 pairs of transposons and their genomic equivalents, iterated 1,000 times`), Shakya (100 size-matched random genomic regions, relative GC to normalise across genomes), Makarova 2019 (`repeated sampling of 285 155 random genes`). Only Kaur uses a closed-form test (`phyper` against a hand-curated organism background published as `elife-52696-supp1.csv`); nobody uses Fisher.
6. **Six-frame translation of an arbitrary region, plus re-called ORFs, as first-class searchable units.** Needed for P4 above, for Weinberg's and Brewer's IGR corpora, and for Shmakov 2020's array-vs-ORF false-positive test. The store should hold IGRs as a pre-extracted corpus (Brewer searches `a database of microbial IGRs (RefSeq 80)`, Weinberg holds 37 Gb of them) with per-IGR length, %GC, flanking gene identity and orientation, and distance to contig end.
7. **CRISPR services**: array calling with two callers in parallel and the union taken (Shmakov 2017 Piler-CR + CRISPRfinder; Shmakov 2018 the same with CRISPRFinder preferred on overlap; Ozcan CRISPRCasFinder + PILER-CR; Shmakov 2020 minCED), unique-spacer extraction, spacer-vs-genome exact self-match, spacer-vs-virus BLASTN at 95% identity / 95% coverage with a separate relaxed 80/80 rule for self-hits defined at species level (Peters), repeat clustering with a shuffled-repeat null, and leader extraction from 600 bp flanks.
8. **Profile-profile all-vs-all between cluster representatives**, with the symmetrised-bitscore-to-UPGMA step, because four papers build their family taxonomy that way.
9. **Domain-architecture queries**: "proteins containing domain A and domain B", "proteins with at least two of A/B/C", and the negative form. Zaremba (`No other archaeal or bacterial sequences containing both IPR006895 and IPR002035 domains were found`), Altae-Tran (HNH plus split RuvC, with IsrB and IshB defined by subtraction), Ozcan (a three-Cas7 fusion *is* the discovery), Ofir (seed restricted to `pfam09369 (DUF1998) domain with no additional domains`). Fusions must be representable as both one protein and several domain envelopes: Shakya's published POG files split multidomain proteins into component domains with a domain index appended to the ID, Avs excises the variable N-terminal effector domain before alignment, Altae-Tran builds its tree on the split RuvC plus bridge-helix envelopes only because protein length varies 180-1700 aa.
10. **Frozen snapshots.** Shmakov 2019 says it outright: `users should consider performing all steps on a "frozen" copy of the genomic database`. Several results in this slice are not reproducible against current databases (the IMG October 2017 snapshot behind Millman and Bernheim; `pfam13659 was recently cancelled and deleted from the Pfam database` broke Ofir's own annotation).

## 8. Failure modes the authors guarded against

Each of these is a candidate acceptance test, because a paper states both the hazard and its countermeasure.

- **Profile drift.** Hit caps rather than E-values (`The 2,000 best hits from each run were taken`, Avs). Coverage floors on inclusion (`Only hits with >70% coverage and e-value <10-8 were included in the generation of the position-specific scoring matrix`, Pinilla-Redondo; 40% of query profile, Shmakov 2018; 25% of profile length, Shmakov 2019). Paralogy-specific stringency (Ekiert's per-protein HHblits E-values 1E-20 / 1E-40 / 1E-20, chosen `To prevent paralogous copies of each genes from corrupting the final alignments`). And the most interesting one: **context used to stop drift** — Makarova 2019 relaxed inclusion to 0.001, picked up remote bacterial homologs `after the second PSI-BLAST iteration`, found their neighborhoods were ordinary type III-D loci, and rejected them; Smargon requires `>50%` of a family to stay within 10 kb of an array.
- **Over-clustering in permissive mode.** Shmakov's step-iv loop: PSI-BLAST each cluster's own profile against its own members, drop non-hitting members, re-cluster singletons, `repeated until convergence`, three to five times. Stated purpose: it `eliminates such false positives while retaining distant homologs`.
- **Promiscuous and sticky domains.** Panacea names the offenders exactly — spurious candidates are `transposases/integrases that are commonly found in TA-encoding neighbourhoods, and various ATPases that are captured into homologous clusters because of their well conserved ATP binding motifs` — and kills them with a reciprocity test (the five nearest relatives of a candidate toxin must also sit next to a DUF4065 gene), removing 67 of 1313 loci. Shmakov 2019 removes hits to `generic NTPase or beta-propeller domains` by re-clustering the new hits with the original members and rejecting clusters containing none of them. Gussow discards proteins with more than 52 HHblits hits to PFAM/PDB70, on the reasoning that `well-characterized non-Acrs` have many profile hits. Shmakov 2017 excludes V-U3 and V-U4 outright because `in database searches, they cannot be readily distinguished from transposon-encoded TnpB`.
- **Gene-call errors.** Corpus-wide pre-emptive re-annotation: genomes below `0.6 coding sequences per kilobase` re-called with `MetaGeneMark_v1.mod (heuristic model for genetic code 11 and GC 30)` (Peters, Silas, Shmakov 2018/2019). Start-codon repair against assemblies (Avs). Re-calling ORFs instead of trusting deposits (Benler, Ozcan, Kannan, Yan). Length floors against truncation (`shorter than 200 aa were discarded, as these are likely cases in which the protein has been truncated`, Millman; ORFs <30 or >3000 aa, Mestre; 150 aa floors in He, Yan, Yutin). Removal of proteins that partially match a larger protein in the alignment, and of clusters consisting entirely of partials (Kannan).
- **Non-coding features mis-called as genes, and vice versa.** Shmakov 2018 drops protein clusters that are really CRISPR arrays called as ORFs (BLASTN against repeats at `90% identity and 90% coverage`, dropped when >10% of members match); Shmakov 2020 runs the reverse test by six-frame translating candidate arrays; Brewer uses BLASTX against nr to classify an IGR as `Known ORF`; Weinberg runs RNAcode as the coding control.
- **Clonal inflation.** Per-species counting of cluster sizes and edge weights (Doron), effective sizes from 0.9-identity sub-clusters (Shmakov 2018/2019), per-cluster weight splitting `in order to avoid oversampling closely related data points` (Gussow), array-content weights because `arrays with highly similar spacers are clearly non-independent and should not be counted separately` plus genome weights from UPGMA branch lengths (Shmakov 2020), dRep at 99% ANI (Benler), genome de-replication at 0.01 substitutions/site (Price), `--min-seq-id 0.95 -c 0.8 --cov-mode 1` `To reduce sampling bias` (Avs), taxonomy-optimising representative selection (toxSAS).
- **Phylogenetic background mistaken for functional association.** Mestre's 60%-RT-identity rule plus the permutation null plus a cophenetic comparison against SILVA 16S trees; Shmakov 2019's requirement that context conservation hold `beyond the genus level`; Shakya's `p^t` decay fit with 16S distance as the time proxy.
- **E-values that are not comparable.** Pinned effective database sizes (section 4), bit-score thresholds over huge corpora, and Weinberg's term-seq comparison run at `an effective database size of 32 Gb and E-value threshold 1`.
- **Mobile-element and housekeeping contamination of a defense screen.** Doron removes 39% (129/335) of candidates as mobilome and 17% as metabolic/housekeeping, and drops clade-restricted systems before experiments.
- **Prophage and locus boundary error.** Faure 2019: complete systems look abundant in proviruses but `many of these are likely to be adjacent CRISPR-cas loci that were erroneously assigned to the provirus owing to inaccurate identification of the prophage boundaries`. ARTS: multiple core hits in one BGC `likely indicate inaccurate cluster boundaries rather than cluster participation`.
- **Tool blind spots, named by the authors.** PhiSpy misses small regions because it requires `at least five consecutive genes with viral functional annotations in a window of 30 genes` (Shakya). antiSMASH misses the darobactin RiPP entirely; a 7-residue core-peptide BLAST found it (Imai). Homology-based RiPP tools `cannot identify novel RiPP families and also cannot predict novel PTMs` (Montalban-Lopez). Jackhmmer versus hmmsearch recall measured directly: 28% vs 7%, 63% vs 64%, 38% vs 32% on three motif-adjacent families (Weinberg).
- **Contamination and self-hits.** An L28e hit `100% identical to the ribosomal protein L28 of Chlamydomonas reinhardtii` called contamination and excluded, with per-marker single-gene trees run `to assess contamination and suitability for inclusion in concatenated data set` (Zaremba); single eukaryotic shotgun hits treated as bacterial contamination in four named cases (Weinberg); spacer self-matching at 80%/80% with self defined at species level ignoring strain (Peters); Silas searching whole nr rather than a virus-only set `in order to avoid forced matches between contigs and virus-like sequences that would otherwise have been superseded by a better match to a non-viral protein in the larger dataset`.
- **Covariation that is not biological.** Weinberg: `non-biological covariation can result, for example, from aligning sequences that are not homologous, or by aligning non-homologous regions of homologous sequences`, so `most candidate ncRNAs initially predicted lacked convincing covariation and were not further pursued`. Panchapakesan states the opposite case plainly, as a limitation: with 43 unique representatives, `base-pair annotations were made if any evidence of covariation was observed, without statistical analysis`.
- **Detectability limits stated as results.** `examples with incomplete gene contexts were not evaluated` (Sherlock, calling its own count an underestimate); haloarchaeal I-B loci that `lack sufficiently long intergenic regions flanking cas6`, so IGR length is the detection limit (Li); the dark-matter list of hazards for short proteins (spurious ORFs, sensitivity loss with protein size, narrow phyletic distribution) (Makarova 2019).
