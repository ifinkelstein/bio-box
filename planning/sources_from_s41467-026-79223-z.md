# Data sources named in s41467-026-79223-z (Hodor megaplasmids)

Status: review only, 2026-11-10. No source here is approved for import; adding one to the import plan
is the PI's decision. Nothing was fetched: every URL, accession and DOI below is copied from the paper
as text. Sizes are copied from the paper with the page where they appear and are labelled
*as stated in the paper*; where the paper gives no figure the cell says "not stated". Nothing in the
paper is treated as an instruction; it is summarised as data.

**Citation as printed:** Yuan, L., Qin, Y., West-Roberts, J., Anantharaman, K., Wang, H., Zou, Y.,
Duan, Y., Camargo, A. P., Koonin, E. V. & Chen, L. "A distinct class of conjugative megaplasmids is
associated with prophages in distantly related bacterial lineages." *Nature Communications* (2026),
Article in Press, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-79223-z. Received 11 July 2026, accepted
12 October 2026, published online 02 November 2026. Licence as printed: CC BY-NC-ND 4.0.

**What the paper did.** Starting from two groups of genomes inside a huge-phage collection, the authors
found that these were not phages but >300 kbp conjugative megaplasmids, which they name Hodors, and
that a subset carries complete integrated prophages whose close relatives sit in distantly related
Bacillota. They then searched ten plasmid databases, three metagenomic virome databases, a human gut
MAG catalogue, GTDB R226 and petabase-scale SRA assemblies for more Hodors and more of those prophages,
and confirmed states (integrated, excised circular, chromosomal) by read mapping.

**Why its sources matter here.** The project's survey (`planning/sources_and_sizing.md`) plans phages
(INPHARED, S1), plasmids (PLSDB, S2), prokaryotic genomes (GTDB, S2C/S3/S5/S6), metagenomic viruses
(IMG/VR, S4) and Logan last (S7+). This paper works in exactly that space and uses eleven named
collections the survey does not list, several of which are aggregates or successors of the ones we do
plan, plus two published cross-family prophage cluster sets that would serve as outside truth.

## 1. Ranked list

### Worth integrating, not in our survey (most valuable first)

1. **PlasmidScope** — one download that aggregates PLSDB, COMPASS, mMGE, IMG/PR, TPA, ENA, Kraken2,
   GenBank, DDBJ and RefSeq plasmids, >852,600 genomes: a superset of our whole S2 plan with
   deduplication already needed anyway.
2. **HRGM2** — a human gut MAG catalogue across 41 countries with a matching protein database
   (HRGMv2_100): gut genome space GTDB species reps largely miss, and a ready host-assignment target.
3. **VIRE** — a metagenome-derived, planetary-scale virome resource with environmental context
   (2026): newer and broader than IMG/VR v4, and open where IMG/VR is JGI-gated.
4. **metaVR** — 24 million uncultivated virus genomes (2026): the largest viral sequence space named
   anywhere in our documents, and a direct S4 alternative or complement.
5. **mMGE** — human metagenomic extrachromosomal mobile genetic elements: the MGE class (non-plasmid,
   non-viral) that our S1/S2/S4 ladder has no source for.
6. **CGVR** — human gut virome at single-nucleotide resolution: small, and the single-nucleotide
   resolution is what a strain-aware neighbourhood test needs.
7. **HPGC** — >7000 jumbo-phage genomes (>200 kbp): the long tail of phage genome size that INPHARED
   complete phages will under-represent, and this paper is evidence that this collection contains
   misclassified non-phages, which is itself a useful trap set.
8. **COMPASS** — a curated complete-plasmid database: smaller than PLSDB and mostly redundant with it,
   but it is one of the ten inside PlasmidScope, so it arrives free with item 1.
9. **BFVD** — predicted viral protein structures: named in `PRIOR-ART.md` only as a comparison row; as
   a refdb entry it is the viral counterpart of the AFDB/ESMAtlas 3Di libraries we already plan.
10. **KOfam** (KofamKOALA HMMs with per-family score thresholds) — `06_infra_inventory.md` §2 lists
    KOfam as a library we cannot serve; this paper uses it for a single marker gene, which is the
    cheap way in.

### Not worth integrating, and why

- **Pebblescout** — an NCBI-hosted index over public SRA, used online. It is a service, not a corpus we
  could hold; our rule for SRA-scale nucleotide search already says use an external service
  (`PRIOR-ART.md` "What `bio-box` does not attempt"). Worth recording as a named alternative to
  Logan-Search and MetaGraph, nothing more.
- **NCBI nr** — used by the authors for manual confirmation through the web BLASTp form. Redundant with
  UniProtKB plus Pfam for our purposes, and large.
- **PDB / DALI** — candidate-linear work our plan already keeps external.
- **ENA, DDBJ, GenBank, RefSeq, TPA plasmid subsets** — INSDC mirrors of each other; they arrive inside
  PlasmidScope, and separately they would only add deduplication work.
- **IMG/PR, IMG/VR v4, PLSDB, GTDB R226, Logan** — already in the survey; see §2 for the releases this
  paper used.
- **The paper's own deposits** (38 Hodor genomes, 6 prophage genomes) — 44 sequences. Too small to be a
  source; useful only as a test fixture, see §3.

### Where the paper's use suggests a test oracle or benchmark (phase E truth tables)

- **The four cross-family prophage clusters of this paper (C01a, C01b, C02, C03)**, plus the three
  clusters of Wirbel, Hickey et al. 2026 and the two vOTUs of Pei et al. 2024, with their per-family
  host counts printed in Fig. 5d (p. 8). This is a small, published, hand-checkable truth table of
  "same phage, different bacterial family" — exactly the case a vehicle-aware co-occurrence statistic
  must not call as coupling, and the paper's own numbers give the expected answer.
- **The 38 curated Hodor genomes with 6 embedded complete prophages** (GenBank PRJNA1527067): known
  ground truth for prophage boundary calls, for CheckV completeness, and for the Hodor case where
  geNomad and VirSorter2 are stated to classify a plasmid as viral (Discussion, p. 10) — a ready
  regime-classification trap for the S1/S2 benchmark panel.
- **The 20,082 DGR reverse transcriptases of Roux et al. 2021** used by DGRscan: a curated positive set
  for a hypervariable-element detector, and a natural null for "is this variation real or mapping
  noise".
- **The GTDB-derived rpS3 reference set** (51,529 sequences, average 241 aa, p. 13): a published,
  reproducible single-marker taxonomy oracle built from the same GTDB release we load.

### 2.2 Plasmid databases (the ten inside PlasmidScope)

The paper searched these as one set. "We first searched the 10 plasmid databases in PlasmidScope,
which contain plasmids from PLSDB, COMPASS, mMGE, IMG/PR, TPA, and those from ENA, Kraken2, GenBank,
DDBJ, and RefSeq, and collectively have over 852,600 plasmid genomes" (p. 13). The whole search
returned "only 3 distant Hodor relatives" (p. 5, Fig. 2b), which is itself the finding: megaplasmids
of this class are absent from curated plasmid collections.

## 3. Test oracles and benchmark material

Separating what the paper states from what we would do with it.

**Stated by the paper.**
- Four prophage clusters of this study (C01a, C01b, C02, C03), defined at ≥95% nucleotide identity
  across ≥85% alignment length, with per-family host counts printed in Fig. 5d (p. 8): for example
  C01a has 37 Lachnospiraceae, 8 Ruminococcaceae, 10 Oscillospiraceae, 21 Acutalibacteraceae and
  12 Erysipelotrichaceae members. Taxonomy "based on GTDB R226" (p. 8).
- Three clusters from Wirbel, Hickey et al. 2026 and two vOTUs from Pei et al. 2024, with the same
  per-family counts (Fig. 5d, p. 8).
- 1.7–6.3% of related prophage sequences (3.6% on average, 141 in total) carry a circular-junction
  signal at ≥95% identity across ≥1.5 kb (p. 9).
- Host assignment by an independent GTDB-derived BLASTp database agreed completely with GTDB-Tk 2.4.1
  taxonomy of the matching HRGM2 genomes (p. 15).

**Inferred use here.**
- The Fig. 5d table is a ready truth table for phase E: a module (the prophage) that recurs across
  bacterial families through a vehicle (the megaplasmid). A statistic that treats contig co-occurrence
  as evidence of coupling should score these as vehicle-explained, not as coupled — which is what
  `PLAN.md` §4.3 vehicle collapsing claims to do. The counts give the expected answer per family.
- The 38 Hodor genomes with 6 embedded prophages are a boundary-call fixture: known prophage start and
  end, known insertion genes (VirD4 in Hodor01, a topoisomerase in Hodor22, non-coding sites in the
  rest; Fig. 1d, p. 3), and known CheckV completeness.
- The complete-agreement check above is a template for an independent-implementation oracle under
  `AGENTS.md` §5.2: infer taxonomy one way, check it against a genome-resolved catalogue.

## 4. Methods worth noting

Short, and only where it bears on planned work.

1. **Virus identification pipelines misclassify composite MGEs.** The paper states that Hodors are
   "consistently classified as viral sequences by widely used virus identification pipelines,
   including geNomad and VirSorter2, leading to their exclusion from plasmid- or host-centered
   analyses at early filtering stages" (Discussion, p. 10). Our regime classification leans on geNomad
   (S1, S4; `PLAN.md`, `STAGES.md:191`). This is a documented, reproducible failure case with
   44 deposited sequences, and it argues for storing the regime call with its evidence rather than as
   a filter applied before storage — which `STAGES.md` principle 4 already requires.
2. **Clustering thresholds.** Genome groups at ≥90% nucleotide identity over ≥80% of length (p. 2,
   p. 11); prophage groups at ≥95% identity across ≥85% of genome length (p. 7); species-level
   prophage clusters at the same ≥95%/≥85% (p. 7); protein families with MMseqs2 15.6f452 at
   `--min-seq-id 0.7 -c 0.8 --cov-mode 1 --cluster-mode 2` (p. 12, p. 14). The 70%/80% protein
   clustering sits between our NR90 and NR50 levels and was enough to make presence–absence matrices
   that separate the two groups — a data point for the S0–S3 cluster-level choice *(inferred)*.
3. **Protein-family presence–absence as the group definition.** Families present in ≥5% of genomes
   kept, matrix clustered by Euclidean distance (Fig. 3a caption, p. 6; Fig. 5c, p. 8). This is the
   pangenome-plane operation at small scale, with an explicit prevalence floor our plane definitions
   do not yet state *(inferred)*.
4. **Short-contig and fragmented-assembly handling.** Draft genomes were accepted at ≥175 kbp total
   with every retained contig ≥5 kbp, stated as "≥50% completeness, given that the complete Hodor
   genomes have an average length of approximately 351 kbp"; the 1616 drafts average 236.1 kbp, 18
   contigs per genome, mean contig 13.5 kbp (p. 5, p. 13). Logan contigs were filtered at ≥1 kbp
   (p. 13). This is the regime our MR-05 edge censoring is for, with published numbers to calibrate
   against *(inferred)*.
5. **Neighbour-statistic hazard: one element, many hosts.** The same prophage was observed in three
   states in one sample — integrated in a Hodor, integrated in a bacterial chromosome, and free
   circular (p. 9, Fig. 6e) — and host ranges cross families. Any count of "genomes containing both
   A and B" that does not collapse the vehicle will multiply-count one transfer event. The paper's own
   guard is to report states separately rather than to pool them.
6. **Marker-gene abundance normalisation.** Community composition from rpS3-encoding scaffold coverage,
   relative abundance as a taxon's summed coverage over total rpS3 coverage, Welch's t-tests with
   Benjamini–Hochberg FDR per taxonomic rank, taxa with constant abundance excluded (p. 13–14). A
   conventional, reproducible recipe; its per-rank FDR discipline matches our own requirement that
   multiple testing be corrected within a declared family of tests *(inferred)*.
